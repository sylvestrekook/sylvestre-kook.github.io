Technology promises productivity, but most tools change *attention* before they change output. The meaningful question for any new tool is not "what can it do?" but "what does it demand of my focus?"

## New versus meaningful

The tech industry measures novelty in features. Meaningfulness is measured in behavior change. A tool is meaningful when it:

- Removes a recurring friction, not just adds a shortcut.
- Changes a habit, not just speeds up an existing one.
- Reduces the number of decisions you must make.

Everything else is novelty — exciting for a week, invisible after.

## AI and the shape of work

AI tools are different from earlier software because they shift work from *execution* to *judgment*. The bottleneck moves from "can I do this?" to "can I tell good from bad?" This raises the value of two things:

1. **Taste and verification** — the ability to judge output a machine produces faster than you can produce it yourself.
2. **Attention management** — if AI removes drudgery, what fills the space determines whether you build or merely consume.

## A rule for adoption

Before adopting any tool, ask: *does this expand what I can create, or does it expand what I have to manage?* Tools that create more maintenance than capability are net negatives, however impressive their demos.

## The human center

Tools are neutral about meaning. They amplify whatever goal you bring to them — including the goal of paying attention well. The most valuable skill of the next decade may simply be the discipline to use powerful tools without letting them use you.
