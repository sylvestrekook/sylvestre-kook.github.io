A common assumption is that faith and reason occupy opposite sides of a line: belief on one side, evidence on the other. This site treats them as complementary — two modes of engaging a reality too large for either to hold alone.

## Reason as a gift

The Christian tradition has never, at its best, asked for the suspension of the mind. The very idea of a rational Creator, of a world ordered and good, is what makes investigation — of nature, of money, of the self — meaningful. To reason carefully *is* a form of reverence for a created order.

## Faith as more than belief

Faith is not belief without evidence. It is trust — a commitment of the whole person toward a reality that reason can point to but not fully contain. It answers questions reason cannot: *why* there is something rather than nothing, what it is *for*, and how to live well in the face of uncertainty and death.

## Where they meet

- **In humility.** Reason shows the limits of what we can prove; faith holds the rest without pretending to certainty.
- **In practice.** Both are tested by living. A belief that cannot be examined and a reason that cannot be trusted both fail the same way — they collapse when pressed.
- **In integration.** The goal is not three separate compartments (finance here, technology there, faith elsewhere) but one coherent life where each informs the others.

## A working posture

I write about faith honestly and openly, subject to the same scrutiny as any other claim. If a position cannot be examined, it does not deserve to be held. If it can be examined and still holds, it is worth living by.

*That posture — honest, examined, integrated — is the thread running through everything on this site.*
